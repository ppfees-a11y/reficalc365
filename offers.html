Compare two refinance offers side by side

Compare your current mortgage and two proposed fixed-rate refinance offers at the same holding period. Evaluate interest, remaining principal and lender costs—not merely the monthly payment.

How to read the comparison: The horizon cost is interest accrued during the holding period plus upfront closing costs. It excludes principal repayments because principal reduces debt; the remaining loan balance is shown separately. All loans are evaluated for the same number of months. For a cash-out refinance, first adjust the proposed principal, and remember that extra cash received changes what you owe.

CSV includes all months up to the selected holding period, and shows zero payments after a loan is paid off. The monthly interest and principal use full-precision calculations, with currency rounded for display. It does not include taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance, adjustable rates, prepayment penalties, tax consequences, or future refinancing.

Why compare the same time horizon?

A new 30-year refinance may lower your payment by stretching repayment longer, while a 15-year offer may have a higher payment and build equity faster. Comparing lifetime interest across loans with different terms can be useful, but it answers a different question than what happens if you sell or refinance in five years.

For an apples-to-apples holding-period comparison, review the interest and upfront costs paid during the period, as well as the remaining principal at the end. The remaining balance matters because it is the amount still owed if you sell or refinance again.

Closing costs and points

Enter the upfront lender and third-party charges you expect to pay for each offer. Do not double-count financed closing costs: if fees are added to the loan principal, they are reflected in the higher principal and should not also be entered as cash paid upfront. Escrow deposits and prepaid taxes are cash needs but are not always equivalent to loan costs.
